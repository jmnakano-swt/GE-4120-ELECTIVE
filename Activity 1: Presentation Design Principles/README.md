
## Activity 1: Free Coffee Poster

### Introduction

For Activity 1, I applied the core principles of presentation design using **Canva** to recreate and develop a simple but effective social media graphic: a **"Free Coffee" poster**.

The design was based on the diagram provided for the activity. Rather than creating the  wording from scratch, I followed the given words and applied the presentation design principles discussed in class.

The poster features the main message:

> **"FREE COFFEE"**

It also includes the tagline provided in the design:

> **"Free coffee. No catch. Just caffeine and good vibes."**

These elements were already part of the given design, so my focus was on applying the appropriate **visual design principles** to organize and present the information effectively.

---

## Design Concept

The main idea behind this activity is based on the principle that:

> **"Design guides attention before content is read."**

When someone looks at a poster, their attention is usually directed toward certain visual elements before they begin reading all of the information.

For this reason, the design emphasizes the main message, **"FREE COFFEE,"** while keeping the supporting information organized and easy to read.

The overall design uses a clean and simple appearance. The black-and-white color scheme helps maintain a professional and uncluttered look while allowing the main message to stand out.

The goal was not to add unnecessary elements, but to make sure that the existing information was presented in a way that was clear, organized, and easy for the audience to understand.

---

# Four Core Principles of Presentation Design

In creating this output, I applied the four core principles of presentation design discussed in class:

1. **Contrast**
2. **Repetition**
3. **Alignment**
4. **Proximity**

These principles helped me organize the elements of the poster and make the information easier to process.

---

## 1. Contrast

The first principle I applied was **Contrast**.

I made sure that the main text, **"FREE COFFEE,"** stood out prominently against the white background. The strong black-and-white contrast immediately attracts the viewer's attention to the most important information.

This follows the principle that:

> **Key elements should be readable and noticeable from across the room.**

The main message is visually stronger than the supporting information, which creates a clear visual hierarchy.

When the viewer first looks at the poster, the large and bold **"FREE COFFEE"** text is one of the first things they notice. After recognizing the main message, they can then look at the other details included in the design.

### How Contrast Improved the Design

Contrast helped:

- Make the main message more noticeable.
- Direct the viewer's attention.
- Improve readability.
- Create a clear visual hierarchy.
- Make the poster easier to understand at a glance.

---

## 2. Repetition

The second principle I applied was **Repetition**.

I maintained consistency by reusing the same font style and color scheme throughout the poster.

The monochrome black-and-white palette and consistent typography make the different elements feel connected rather than appearing as separate or unrelated parts.

Repetition creates consistency and helps the audience **learn to read the design quickly**.

Since the same visual characteristics are repeated throughout the poster, the design feels more cohesive and professional.

### How Repetition Improved the Design

Repetition helped:

- Maintain visual consistency.
- Create a cohesive appearance.
- Make the design look more organized.
- Avoid unnecessary visual distractions.
- Give the poster a more professional appearance.

---

## 3. Alignment

The third principle I applied was **Alignment**.

I made sure that the different elements of the poster were positioned according to a clear structure. The title, date, location, and booth number follow an organized layout rather than being placed randomly.

Using Canva's alignment and grid tools helped me position the elements consistently.

Alignment creates visual order and makes the content easier for the audience to follow.

Instead of forcing the viewer to search around the poster for each piece of information, the organized placement allows their eyes to move naturally from one element to another.

This supports the idea of:

> **Making content easy to process.**

### How Alignment Improved the Design

Alignment helped:

- Create a clean and organized layout.
- Establish visual structure.
- Make information easier to scan.
- Prevent elements from appearing randomly placed.
- Make the poster look more polished and professional.

---

## 4. Proximity

The fourth principle I applied was **Proximity**.

I grouped information that belongs together so that the relationship between the different pieces of information is immediately clear.

For example, the **event details**, including the date, time, and location, are grouped together.

Meanwhile, the call-to-action:

> **"First come, First served"**

is positioned separately at the bottom.

This allows the audience to quickly distinguish the event information from the call-to-action.

Proximity helps reduce confusion because related information is visually connected, while unrelated information is given enough space to appear separate.

### How Proximity Improved the Design

Proximity helped:

- Group related information together.
- Separate different types of information.
- Reduce visual confusion.
- Improve readability.
- Make important details easier to locate.
- Create a more organized composition.

---

# Overall Effect of Applying the Principles

Applying these four principles improved the effectiveness of the poster because each design element has a clear visual purpose.

The poster does not require the audience to read every detail before understanding its main message. The design itself guides the viewer through the information.

### Faster Comprehension

The first major benefit is **faster comprehension**.

The viewer can immediately recognize that the poster is about **free coffee** because the main message is emphasized through size, contrast, and placement.

The supporting information can then be read afterward.

### Better Retention

The second benefit is **better retention**.

The clean and consistent design makes the main message visually distinct and easier to remember.

The limited color palette, consistent typography, and organized layout prevent unnecessary distractions and allow the audience to focus on the important information.

### Better Organization

The use of alignment and proximity also makes the information easier to navigate.

Related details are grouped together, while different sections are separated appropriately. This makes the poster feel structured rather than crowded.

---

# Reflection

This activity helped me understand that presentation design is not simply about making a poster look attractive.

It is also about understanding **how people see and process information**.

By applying Contrast, Repetition, Alignment, and Proximity, I learned how small design decisions can affect the way an audience understands a message.

I also learned that a designer does not always need to add more elements to make a design effective. Sometimes, keeping the design simple and organizing the existing elements properly can communicate the message more effectively.

In this activity, the main content and wording were provided in the original design. My focus was on applying the design principles to ensure that these elements were presented clearly and effectively.

---

# Key Takeaway

This activity demonstrated how the four principles of **Contrast, Repetition, Alignment, and Proximity** can improve visual communication.

- **Contrast** directs attention to important information.
- **Repetition** creates consistency.
- **Alignment** creates structure and order.
- **Proximity** shows relationships between related information.

Together, these principles make the poster easier to understand, more organized, and more visually effective.

Ultimately, I learned that **effective presentation design is not just about how a design looks, but about how effectively it communicates information to the audience.**
